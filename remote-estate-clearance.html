Remote estate clearance

How to manage a property clearance when you cannot attend in person.

Many families need to manage a property from interstate or overseas. A remote clearance process gives you visibility, control and documentation without needing to attend every visit in person.

Quick takeaway: Remote clearance works best when everything is documented: photos before work, agreed item categories, progress updates and completion images.

When remote estate clearance is useful

Remote clearance is common when adult children live interstate, executors are overseas, family members cannot travel, or the property needs to be prepared quickly for sale. The goal is to remove uncertainty by replacing site visits with clear communication and photo evidence.

  • Interstate executors managing a Melbourne, Sydney or Brisbane property.
  • Families coordinating work around probate, settlement or auction dates.
  • Beneficiaries who need to review items before removal.
  • Agents who need a property cleared before photography or inspections.

How the process works

  1. Initial call: discuss the property, access, family concerns and timing.
  2. Photo or video review: use photos, video walkthroughs or agent access to estimate scope.
  3. Written quote: confirm what will be removed, kept, donated, cleaned or prepared.
  4. Item review: identify valuables, documents, photos and sentimental items.
  5. Onsite work: complete clearance, donation, disposal and optional cleaning.
  6. Completion report: provide final photos and invoice for estate records.

What photos should you send?

Good photos can reduce delays and make quoting easier. Stand in the doorway of each room and take wide photos. Include cupboards, wardrobes, garage, sheds, outdoor areas and any difficult access points.

AreaWhat to capture
Living areasFurniture volume, boxes, bookshelves and general contents.
BedroomsWardrobes, beds, drawers and personal items to check.
KitchenAppliances, cupboards, pantry and cleaning condition.
Garage/shedTools, chemicals, heavy items, shelving and vehicle access.
OutsideGreen waste, outdoor furniture, paths, side access and garden condition.

Protecting sentimental and valuable items remotely

The biggest worry for remote families is that something important may be thrown out. The best solution is a staged process. Items that might be valuable or sentimental are photographed, separated and confirmed before disposal.

  • Create a “must keep” list before work starts.
  • Nominate a family decision-maker to avoid conflicting instructions.
  • Ask for photos of uncertain items before they are removed.
  • Use labelled boxes for documents, photos, jewellery, medals or keepsakes.
  • Arrange courier, storage or collection for retained items.

Access and keys

Remote jobs need a clear access plan. Keys may be held by an agent, neighbour, solicitor or family member. If the property is vacant, confirm security and whether utilities are still connected before cleaning or inspections.

What should be included in a remote completion report?

  • Before photos or initial room references.
  • Progress notes for major stages.
  • Photos of retained or uncertain items.
  • Final room-by-room completion photos.
  • Details of any issues discovered onsite.
  • Itemised invoice suitable for estate records.
Practical tip: Remote clearance is smoother when one person is authorised to make decisions. Too many approval points can delay the job and increase cost.
