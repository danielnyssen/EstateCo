Downsizing property clearance guide

A calm, practical guide for downsizing, aged care transitions and whole-home sorting.

Downsizing is rarely just a removal job. It is a careful process of deciding what to keep, what to pass on, what to store and how to make the next home safe, comfortable and manageable.

Quick takeaway: The best downsizing projects start with the new home. Work backwards from available space, daily routines and what the person truly needs.

Why downsizing feels difficult

For many families, downsizing means sorting decades of belongings in a short time. There may be emotional attachment, family disagreement, physical limitations and pressure from aged care, settlement or lease dates. A calm process reduces stress and prevents rushed decisions.

Start with the destination

Before clearing the current home, understand the next home. A retirement village unit, aged care room or smaller apartment may not have space for large furniture, outdoor equipment, multiple sets of crockery or years of stored paperwork.

The keep, gift, donate, sell, store and remove method

Use simple categories. Complicated systems slow the process down and create uncertainty.

  • Keep: daily-use furniture, clothing, medication items, photos, important documents and favourite possessions.
  • Gift: items family members want and can collect by a set date.
  • Donate: clean, usable furniture, clothing and household items.
  • Sell: valuable items where selling is worth the time and effort.
  • Store: short-term items where a decision is not yet possible.
  • Remove: broken, unsafe, unsanitary or unwanted items.

How to make decisions easier

Ask practical questions rather than emotional ones. “Do you want to keep this?” can be overwhelming. “Will this fit in the new bedroom?” or “Do you use this every week?” is easier to answer.

Family tip: Give relatives a clear deadline to collect items. Without deadlines, downsizing can stall for weeks and create extra storage or property costs.

What to prioritise for aged care moves

  • Comfortable clothing and labelled personal items.
  • Medical documents, prescriptions and mobility aids.
  • Family photos, small keepsakes and familiar décor.
  • Toiletries, glasses, hearing aids and chargers.
  • A small number of meaningful items rather than boxes of rarely used belongings.

Preparing the property after downsizing

Once the person has moved, the remaining property often needs a full clearance. This may include furniture, old appliances, garage contents, sheds, garden waste and years of stored household goods. If the home is being sold, combine clearance with cleaning and basic property preparation.

Common downsizing mistakes

  • Moving too much into the new home and creating clutter immediately.
  • Leaving family decisions open-ended.
  • Paying for storage without a clear review date.
  • Assuming charities can collect everything.
  • Forgetting garages, sheds, roof storage and outdoor areas.

A calmer downsizing timeline

  1. Four to six weeks before move: measure the new space and choose essential furniture.
  2. Two to four weeks before move: sort personal items and arrange family collection.
  3. Move week: relocate essentials and make the new room feel familiar.
  4. After move: clear remaining property contents and prepare the home for sale, rent or handover.
