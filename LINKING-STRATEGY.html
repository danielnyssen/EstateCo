Linking principles that build credibility

Why this structure works

Google evaluates pages partly by how many other pages on your site link to them — and whether those links use relevant anchor text. The structure here creates a three-tier link graph:

1
Home + Melbourne page → all 7 regional hubs
High-authority root pages distributing link equity to regional hubs
2
Each regional hub → its suburb pages
Hub pages pass authority to individual suburb pages in their area
3
Each suburb page → nearby suburbs + its hub + Melbourne page
Suburb pages link laterally (nearby) and upward (hub, Melbourne, home)

Anchor text rules

  • Use "Estate Clearance [Suburb]" as anchor text — matches search queries
  • Vary occasionally: "deceased estate cleanout [Suburb]", "house clearance [Suburb]"
  • Don't use generic "click here" or "learn more"
  • Footer links should use the suburb name only (shorter)

What makes these pages credible

  • Unique content per page (local council, aged care references, local context)
  • Schema LocalBusiness + Service + BreadcrumbList + FAQPage
  • Breadcrumb navigation (Home → Melbourne → Suburb)
  • Canonical tags on every page
  • Internal links flowing in both directions

Linking your guides content to suburb pages

Your existing guides are valuable — they create topical authority. Link from guides to relevant suburb pages contextually. For example:

  • In a guide about "executor responsibilities" → link to "Estate Clearance Melbourne" and "Estate Clearance [high-search suburb]"
  • In a guide about "how to prepare a property for sale" → link to "Pre-Sale Property Preparation" service and 2–3 suburb pages
  • Each guide should link to at least 2–3 suburb or service pages naturally within the body text
Pro tip: Add a "We service this area" callout box at the end of each guide with links to 3–4 suburb pages — this creates high-quality contextual links from informational content to service pages.
